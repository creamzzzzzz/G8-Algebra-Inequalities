outcomes 1, 2 and 3

Index laws

An index tells you how many times a base is multiplied by itself. In a5, the base is a and the index is 5. The laws only work when the bases are the same.

Multiplying: add the indices

am × an = am + n
Textbook: Ex 2A, p18 to 19

Dividing: subtract the indices

am ÷ an = am − n
Coefficients are multiplied or divided as normal.

Zero index

a0 = 1
Any non-zero base. Only the base with the zero is affected: 5x0 = 5.

Negative index

a−n = 1 ÷ an
A negative index means “flip to the other side of the fraction”. It does not make the answer negative.

Index explorer

Try dividing with equal indices to see the zero index appear, then make the second index bigger to see a negative index.

Worked examples

Multiply
3x2 × 4x5
  1. 1
    3 × 4 = 12
    Multiply the coefficients.
  2. 2
    x2 × x5 = x2 + 5 = x7
    Same base, so add the indices.
  3. 3
    12x7
    Put the pieces together.
Divide, with a zero index
15a6b ÷ 5a2b
  1. 1
    15 ÷ 5 = 3
    Divide the coefficients.
  2. 2
    a6 − 2 = a4
    Same base, so subtract the indices.
  3. 3
    b1 − 1 = b0 = 1
    A lone b has index 1. The zero index gives 1.
  4. 4
    3a4
    The 1 disappears.
Negative index
6x5y−2 ÷ 3x2y−5
  1. 1
    6 ÷ 3 = 2
    Divide the coefficients.
  2. 2
    x5 − 2 = x3
    Subtract the x indices.
  3. 3
    y−2 − (−5) = y−2 + 5 = y3
    Subtracting a negative is adding.
  4. 4
    2x3y3
    The final index is already positive.
Write with positive indices
4x−3
  1. 1
    x−3 = 1 ÷ x3
    Only the x has the negative index. The 4 stays where it is.
  2. 2
    4 × 1 ÷ x3 = 4 ÷ x3
    Multiply back by the coefficient.

Collecting like terms

Like terms have exactly the same variable part, so 3x and −x are like terms but x and x2 are not. Add or subtract the coefficients and keep each sign with its term. Textbook: Ex 2B, p20.

Simplify
x terms y terms numbers
Keep the sign with the termIn 3x + 2y − x the term is −x, so 3x − x = 2x.
Check the whole variable part2a2 and 5a cannot be combined. Neither can xy and x.

Product of two linear expressions

To expand (a + b)(c + d), multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second, then collect like terms. Four products, then simplify. Textbook: Ex 2E, p23 and Ex 16B, p281.

Blue boxes are positive products and pink boxes are negative. Watch the signs: a negative times a positive is negative, and a negative times a negative is positive.

Worked example
(2x − 3)(x + 4)
  1. 1
    2x × x = 2x2
    First × first.
  2. 2
    2x × 4 = 8x
    First × second.
  3. 3
    −3 × x = −3x
    Second × first.
  4. 4
    −3 × 4 = −12
    Second × second.
  5. 5
    2x2 + 8x − 3x − 12 = 2x2 + 5x − 12
    Collect the like terms 8x − 3x.
Checks before you finish 1. Did you write four products? 2. Is the sign of each product correct? 3. Did you collect the two middle terms? 4. Is the answer in order: squared term, x term, number?

Substituting into expressions

Replace each letter with its number, and put every substituted number in brackets. Brackets keep negatives safe, especially with powers. Follow the order of operations: brackets, indices, multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. Textbook: Ex 2G, p25.

1 · Substitute with brackets
2 · Evaluate
The sign trap(−3)2 = 9 but −32 = −9. The brackets decide whether the sign is squared.
Two negatives−2 × (−5) = +10 and 6 − (−4) = 10. Subtracting a negative is adding.
outcomes 7, 8 and 9

Inequalities

An inequality compares two values instead of saying they are equal. The solution is usually a whole range of numbers, shown on a number line.

The four signs (outcome 7)

<less thanx < 3 means x is below 3, and 3 is not included.
>greater thanx > 3 means x is above 3, and 3 is not included.
≤less than or equal tox ≤ 3 includes 3 itself.
≥greater than or equal tox ≥ 3 includes 3 itself.

On a number line, an open circle means the number is not included and a closed circle means it is. The arrow points in the direction of the solutions.

Number line builder

Solving linear inequalities (outcome 8)

Solve it like an equation: do the same to both sides. There is one extra rule.

Flip the signWhen you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, the inequality sign reverses. So −2x > 8 becomes x < −4. Textbook: Ex 16I, p288.

    Worked example
    A gym charges a $20 joining fee plus $5 per visit. Sara can spend at most $65. How many visits can she make?
    1. 1
      Let v = the number of visits.
    2. 2
      20 + 5v ≤ 65
      “At most” means ≤.
    3. 3
      5v ≤ 45
      Subtract 20 from both sides.
    4. 4
      v ≤ 9
      Divide by 5. The sign stays because 5 is positive.
    5. 5
      Sara can make at most 9 visits.
      Check: 20 + 5 × 9 = 65, which is within her budget.
    Worked example
    I think of a number, triple it, then subtract 4. The result is at least 20. What can the number be?
    1. 1
      Let n = the number.
    2. 2
      3n − 4 ≥ 20
      “At least” means ≥.
    3. 3
      3n ≥ 24
      Add 4 to both sides.
    4. 4
      n ≥ 8
      Divide by 3.
    5. 5
      The number is 8 or greater.

    Exam rules and vocabulary

    No electronic devices

    A calculator, Apple Watch, smartwatch or any other electronic device is not allowed.

    Positive indices only

    Unless the question says otherwise, final answers must contain only positive indices. Rewrite x−2 as 1 ÷ x2.

    Always show working

    You are strongly encouraged to show working every time. When a question asks you to show it, an answer without clear, detailed algebraic working earns no mark.

    Move on, then return

    Questions get harder. If you are stuck, leave it, answer one you can, and come back if there is time.

    Vocabulary

    index
    The small raised number showing how many times the base is multiplied. Plural: indices.x4 = x × x × x × x
    like terms
    Terms with exactly the same variable part. Only these can be added or subtracted.5y and −2y
    expression
    A mix of numbers, variables and operations with no equals or inequality sign.3x + 2y − 7
    variable
    A letter that stands for a number that can change.x, y, a, b
    coefficient
    The number multiplied by a variable.In −6x2 the coefficient is −6.
    inequality
    A statement comparing two values with <, >, ≤ or ≥.2x + 1 ≤ 9

    Command terms

    simplify
    Write in the shortest form by collecting like terms or using index laws.4a + 3a = 7a
    expand
    Multiply out brackets.(x + 2)(x + 3) = x2 + 5x + 6
    factorize
    Write as a product by taking out a common factor. The reverse of expanding.6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3)
    substitute
    Replace a letter with a given number.3a when a = −2 is 3(−2) = −6
    calculate
    Work out a numerical answer, showing the steps. With no calculator, do it by hand.
    solve
    Find the value, or the range of values, that makes the statement true.x + 3 > 7 gives x > 4
